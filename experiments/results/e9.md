# E9: what stops a meter that lies?

Generated by `npm run e9` on 2026-09-28: 13th Gen Intel(R) Core(TM) i5-13450HX (16 threads), win32 10.0.26200, Node v22.10.0, OpenSSL 3.0.15+quic. Run time 970.1 s.

Masking hides each input, so a meter can report any value and the published total moves by its lie. Two defences: plausibility checks on each published total (the default), and verified mode, in which each report carries a Pedersen commitment on ristretto255 and a 16-bit range proof (readings from -32768 to 32767 W), the coordinator checks every total against the sum of the commitments, and validators can check the proofs.

## Plausibility checks

Bounds come from one simulated summer day of 200 homes at 10-second rounds (`src/load.ts`): the group's mean load per home ranged from 184.8 to 744.8 W and moved at most 39.09 W per home between rounds. The bounds widen the range by a quarter on each side and double the step: mean per home in [44, 885] W, step at most 79 W per home (`PLAUSIBLE` in src/params.ts; derived again here: same).

On a second simulated day with no liar, 0 of 8640 rounds were flagged. Then one meter lies for 180 rounds (30 minutes) from a random start, 60 times per size: suddenly, or ramping up over 10 minutes. A lie is caught if any of its rounds is flagged.

## Verified mode

Group of 50 at E7's k = 32, t = 17; one meter lies in every round. "Out of range" adds 50000 W, which no 16-bit proof can cover; "within range" adds 5000 W and proves it honestly.

| the liar | proofs checked by the coordinator | rounds | aborted | liar left out | lie published | of those, refused by validators checking every proof |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| changes its masked value only | 100.0% | 10 | 10 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| claims an out-of-range reading | 100.0% | 10 | 0 | 10 | 0 | 0 |
| claims an out-of-range reading | 10.0% | 100 | 0 | 9 | 91 | 91 |
| claims a false reading within range | 100.0% | 10 | 0 | 0 | 10 | 0 |

A meter that changes its masked value without its commitment makes the aggregate check fail, whatever share of proofs is checked. A meter that claims an out-of-range reading is left out when its proof is checked; when the coordinator samples proofs, the lies it misses are published and validators that check every proof refuse to record them. A false reading within range is a valid reading as far as any proof can tell.

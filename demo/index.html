03 Why not just encrypt it?

Encryption hides the road, not the destination.

Readings are already encrypted on their way. But the utility holds the key, so on its servers every reading is back in the clear, ready to be stored, analysed or shared.

Removing names doesn't help much either: the utility still has to know which meter sits behind which transformer.

And the stakes are real. Under India's data protection law, failing to safeguard personal data can cost up to ₹250 crore[S8].

04 How Veil works

Five neighbours, five readings.

Take five houses on this lane. Each meter has its reading for the round, shown on its tag.

1

Neighbours share secret masks

One adds it, one takes it away.

Every pair of neighbours agrees a random number. One adds it to its reading, the other subtracts it. No one else knows it.

reading+ mask− mask
2

Each meter sends noise

What leaves the house means nothing alone.

A report is the reading plus its masks. On its own it tells the utility nothing about the home.

reading + masks= noise
3

The sum is exact

Every mask meets its opposite.

The utility adds the reports. The masks cancel and vanish, leaving the true total, to the watt.

Its report changes, the masks stay the same, and the total still comes out exact.

Illustrative: 5 houses, 2 neighbours each, 4-digit masks, real simulated readings.

Veil runs 240 homes with 54 neighbours each[soak] and fresh masks every round, all sums mod 2^64[rounds].

05 When a meter drops

Meters go offline. Rounds go on.

If a house misses the round, the masks it shared don't cancel, and the sum turns to noise.

Recovery

Its neighbours fix the sum.

They release just what cancels the masks they shared with it, and the utility gets the exact total of everyone who reported.

Releases happen only after the meters agree on who reported, and unmasking a home takes t of its k neighbours. In the attacks we ran, fewer than t colluding neighbours learned nothing[attacks].

Veil is built for 10% of meters missing a round[params], above the 8.9% and 6.9% daily loss measured on meters in Mathura and Bareilly[dropout].

06 Keeping the utility honest

Every total goes on a ledger.

The utility signs each total with the evidence behind it, and a group of validators each keep a copy. A second, different total for the same round is refused, and an auditor can recompute any total from the signed evidence.

08 Errata

What it doesn't do.

  • A lying meter is caught only in part. Plausibility checks catch a sudden lie of 20 kW or more[E9]. Verified mode catches out-of-range lies, but a false reading inside the range still passes.
  • Heavy dropout stops rounds. With 30% of meters missing, 7 of 20 rounds published; at 40%, none[E4].
  • The total still says something. That is why a group needs at least 200 homes, a number set by one attack on simulated homes[E5].
  • Browsers are slow at this. Setting up 240 meters in pure JavaScript took 57.8 s in our soak run[soak].

The back cover About Veil

A privacy layer for smart-meter data. The grid gets its totals; homes keep their diaries.

Veil is a protocol and an open codebase. Meters mask their readings in pairs, a coordinator adds the reports, and the masks cancel into the exact total of a transformer's homes. Nobody, the utility included, ever holds one home's reading.

Questions people ask

Can the utility still bill me?

Yes. Bills need each home's energy, so they go on a separate, coarse channel, as the billing register records it. The fine-grained readings that reveal a routine are the ones Veil masks.

What if my meter goes offline?

Its neighbours release just what cancels the masks they shared with it, and the round publishes the exact total of everyone who reported. Veil is built for 10% of meters missing a round[params].

Could the utility publish a fake total?

Not quietly. Each total goes on a ledger that a group of validators keep, a second total for the same round is refused, and an auditor can recompute any total from the signed evidence.

Could a meter lie about its reading?

In part. Plausibility checks catch a sudden lie of 20 kW or more[E9]. Verified mode catches out-of-range lies; a false reading inside the range still passes.

Doesn't the total itself leak something?

A little, which is why Veil publishes only totals of large groups. On a total of 200 homes, a water-pump detector does about as well as a coin toss[E5].

Is this deployed anywhere?

Not yet. Veil is a research build: the protocol, the experiments and this demo are complete and open, and the next steps are timings on meter hardware and a pilot.
